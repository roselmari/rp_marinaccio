The Effect of the Chemical Glyphosate on the Growth of Chlamydomonas (Green Algae) and Lemna
Minor (Duckweed) Within One Tank

Made by Rose Marinaccio with mentor Craig Queenan

Background & Rationale

Chlamydomonas (green algae) and Lemna minor (duckweed) can both become invasive species in the right conditions, causing a loss of biodiversity and choking out other plants. Duckweed has also been known to take nutrients from the water around it, oftentimes covering the surface of water entirely and blocking out sunlight. Algae is a vital part to many aquatic areas, but can become algal blooms and kill off species of fish with chemical imbalances after dying. The herbicide glyphosate can provide a solution to this by killing off the algae. However, it is known for often affecting other organisms in the area as well as the target. Glyphosate's effect on algae includes inhibiting the process of photosynthesis, effectively preventing the growth and spread of the plant. Without a way to get energy, the algae body will eventually die out and leave the ecosystem.

Objective & Explanation

Objective

  • Understand how duckweed would grow alongside algae
  • Understand how the herbicide glyphosate would affect them together
Independent variable
  • The presence of glyphosate in each tube
  • Which plants were in the experiment
Dependent variable
  • The growth or retraction of green algae
  • The color and appearance of duckweed

Materials & Methods

The experiment was conducted at the HTHS Research Lab, Lincroft, New Jersey. Six disposable 50mL tubes were used for each trial of the experiment, with different conditions in each. The tubes were located underneath grow lights for 8 hours each day and monitored over a course of 5 days, with the collection of data occurring between 11am and 12pm once every two days. A hemocytometer was used along with a microscope from Carolina Biological for determining the density of the algae. The green algae for the experiment was procured from Carolina Biological, with 5 milliliters (mL) of the algae solution from the stock tank added to the appropriate tubes. The concentration of the algae was recorded on the first day of the trials and varied based on the growth of the stock tank of algae. The duckweed was also bought from Carolina Biological, with 20 ± 5 fronds of the duckweed added to the appropriate tubes. The final glyphosate solution was used in doses of 5 mL of 5% glyphosate per tube.
